# GOALS — what the army is for, what to fix next, what was changed
Owner's long-term goal: develop this survival world endlessly with 50 bots — iron → diamonds → Nether → Ender Dragon, plus
mega builds (castle/tenshu). Legit survival only. WORLD 2 (new seed, 09-19): humans are SPECTATORS — they watch and chat, nothing else;
no PvP, no looting, no raids. The only enemies are mobs, terrain and our own mistakes. World 1's history: `attic/world1/` (local only, see its README).

## Scale rule (owner, 09-19): this is a 50-player survival (30 until 09-19, owner: "50体でいこう") — size EVERYTHING x50
A solo player's base has 5 chests, a 9x9 field and one furnace; ours needs ~200 containers, fields of 1000+ cells, furnace banks, squads of 6-14 on
every work, roads instead of paths. When a number looks fine for one player, multiply it by 50 before you put it on the board.

## Doctrine — optimise the army's next 1000 trips, not this bot's current trip
The owner's diagnosis of the first attempt: the AI could not give the *ordinary good sense* of a Minecraft player. Each bot did what
was cheapest for itself right now (dig through, pillar over, leave the hole, farm on ice) and the sum was a wrecked, hostile base.
A good player — and a kind one — invests in the shared world, because everyone, including itself tomorrow, pays for every flaw again
and again. Judge every order and every line of bot code by these five questions:
1. **Does it make the place better or worse for the next one who comes by?** Holes get filled (yours, a creeper's, anybody's), scaffolds
   removed, trees felled whole and replanted, gates closed, paths lit. Never leave a trap to save ten seconds.
2. **Will this obstacle be met again?** Then fix it ONCE, properly, for everybody: a reusable 2-wide stair where there is a cliff, a bridge over
   the gap, a road on the daily route, a chest + table at the work site. Cost of the fix vs (trips × detour/risk) — commons win almost always.
   Never a private shortcut: movement is read-only, and what a bot cannot pass it reports (`no_route`, `terrain_debt.jsonl`).
3. **Are we fighting the land instead of choosing it?** The world is huge and free. Farm where water is liquid and the ground is flat and warm;
   keep animals where grass grows; mine where the ore is; build where it is beautiful. Scout first (`scout` job → `armyctl.js sites`), then
   settle there — an outpost with its own chest, bed and farm, joined to the base by a road. Walking 300 blocks once beats freezing for days.
4. **Is someone already providing this?** One quartermaster cooks, crafts tools and keeps the chests sorted so 29 others don't each open every
   chest. One stair, one mine entrance, one chest index. Specialise and share; don't duplicate.
5. **Is everybody working? (owner: 「全員動かして、数の暴力」)** Thirty bots are the army's one real advantage — use it. Think in squads of
   6-12 on BIG works (a huge branch mine, a huge farm, a lumber camp, filling the crater, roads), not in single errands. A bot standing at
   muster by day is a planning failure: the only good reasons are unfit (hp/food), night on the surface, or a missing tool — and a missing
   tool is itself a job (toolsmith). Big does not mean messy: every squad job still has a zone, a plan and read-only movement.

## Standing rules (short version of the old DIRECTIVE/BASICS — every job handler obeys them)
- **Level first (owner 09-19).** No construction on raw terrain: the `build` job pads every blueprint by itself (footprint+1 levelled, 4 headroom; `params.pad:false` / terrain-spanning blueprints opt out); fields and yards get a `level` build before use. One site = one height.
- **Routes are reversible.** Surface trips step down max 1 block (a 3-drop only towards base on the retry) — a route a bot cannot walk back is a trap.
1. **Output over motion.** A bot that cannot do its job (no tool, no food, 1 hp) stands at muster; it does not wander or circle chests.
2. **Squads, not errands.** Up to `maxFronts` (6) work sites at once, each staffed by a squad at the same site; nobody wanders alone.
3. **One implementation of each basic** (travel, chest I/O, eat, craft, smelt) — in `lib/army.js` & friends. No copies.
4. **Base logistics belong to the quartermaster job**, not to every worker: workers bank raw output and draw kit by the chest index.
5. **Movement is read-only; world edits need a plan + zone** (docs/DEV.md §4, docs/WORLD.md zones).
6. **keep_inventory is OFF**: bank valuables at every base visit; night work only underground or inside the lit base.
7. Never hand-dig stone (no drop, 7 s/block); never dig straight down; never x-ray (mine only ore you exposed).
8. In-game chat is never an order (humans are spectators; the chat daemon only talks).

## Lessons from world 1 (paid for in full — do not pay twice)
1. **Site choice decides everything.** A base in snowy taiga had no liquid water, no animals, no cane; every job fought the land. Scout FAR (400-800+ blocks) before settling; walking once beats freezing for days.
2. **One site = ONE height; level first.** The mega farm on a y86/y87 mosaic could never be watered; decks over voids and craters swallowed bots. `level` / `fill_void` before building; never deck a hole.
3. **Never trust an event without looking at the world.** "plugged", "planted", "build_done" lied (client prediction, stale counters). Read results back from the server and LOOK (`look`, `mapshot.js`) before believing a number.
4. **Never break a non-empty container.** Removing/moving chests destroyed stock; empty it first, verify, then break.
5. **Irrigation = closed, capped holes AT FIELD LEVEL.** A source above ground or an open hole floods the field; water below the soil level hydrates nothing.
6. **Audit what we built.** Compare the world with the blueprint / the planted count on every visit (`structure_damaged`, `hedge_damaged`, `crops_vanished`, `farm_degrading`, `flood`, `void_under_pad`); our own jobs undo each other more often than anybody else.
7. **Long-range scouting is a job, not an accident.** Biomes, herds, water, villages come from `scout` squads on rotating bearings; without them the army did not know what was 300 blocks away.
8. **Code is written only by the top model (Fable), one owner per file.** Operators and the foreman steer the board and file evidence in docs/BUGS.md; six authors once wrote six frameworks.
9. **Atomic saves, checked edits.** Every shared file is written tmp+rename; `ops/check.sh` after every edit; never a `//` comment on a chained line (it ate the dispatcher's `.sort()` for 40 min).
10. **Waiting is waste, idling is a planning failure.** Sponge jobs with big head-counts, shifts for long commutes, plans that free their bot when done — nobody waits for an LLM.
